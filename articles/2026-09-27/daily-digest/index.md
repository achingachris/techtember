this week's crawl produced five editions, and if you read all of them back to back (I did, so you don't have to), a few threads run through every single one. some of it is boring in the good way: Kenya's internet and payments infrastructure keeps getting sturdier without much drama. some of it is not boring at all: the industry racing to ship autonomous AI agents is discovering, in public, that those agents are also a new kind of attack surface. and somewhere in the middle, Apple finally admitted foldable phones are a category worth entering, years after everyone else did.

## kenya: the numbers everyone quoted, and why they matter

every edition this week (edition-1 through edition-5) opened with the same three [techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/mobile-money-subscriptions-reach-54-million/) data points, and for good reason. mobile money subscriptions hit 54 million accounts by June 2026, up 13.2% year over year, with Safaricom's M-Pesa still sitting at 88.8% market share. that share barely moved, which tells you the real competition in Kenyan fintech isn't for mobile money dominance anymore; it's for what gets built on top of it.

mobile subscriptions overall rose 4.6% to 88 million, pushing penetration to 165% ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/mobile-subscriptions-kenya-88-million/)), which just means most of us carry two SIMs and call it a personality trait. the number worth building around, though, is fixed internet: subscriptions surged 32% to 2.84 million, driven by fiber rollout and Starlink filling gaps that fixed-line telephony will never fill again ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/)). if you're planning capacity for a Kenyan user base, that fiber and satellite growth matters more than the mobile headline. mobile gets you reach; fiber gets you bandwidth for anything beyond basic API calls and payment webhooks.

layered on top of that, a Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) report found local banks adopting AI, application programming interfaces (APIs), cloud infrastructure, and digital lending fast enough that the line between "bank" and "fintech" is disappearing ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/)). edition-3 and edition-5 both flagged the same implication for engineers: more banks exposing APIs means more integration work, and more regulatory scrutiny on how that data moves. Nairobi hosted its first WSO2Con Africa this same week, putting API and enterprise architecture vendors in the same room as that shift ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/25/wso2con-africa-2026-nairobi/)).

## agentic AI's security problem stopped being theoretical

if there's one story that got sharper with every edition, it's this one. Microsoft published research on Storm-3168, a threat actor running agentic-driven cloud attacks by chaining together stolen service principal credentials, letting autonomous agents move through cloud environments faster than a human attacker ever could ([Microsoft via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizwFBVV95cUxNM092d3lCWFRTMmMxZ09DODM4Z3hxWUNzSV93OVFoYUU3T2tYTUxudnMyc2MzR1R0QU1QSEI2ZzJQRjNfaUNVOUVRUU9LLTNSalE4SkEyM0FWcHN0MDlJb2dwcU8zNUhtbDYySjJLU3VYaVBvb1lLQUI2T2huNTduTkRiQjRYcWJ4WG9HNGpZSFNrbW5iSDVLSVdwUXpmRUdQTWt4RHVfUm5RQ1NJaDBKYWZjMDdNY0RGREpSeWZUeVlsVmd0eVJXZS1PM2xqWkU?oc=5)). the market noticed fast: cybersecurity startup Island was valued at $6.4 billion, explicitly tied to concerns about AI agent security risk ([Reuters via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipgFBVV95cUxPd1puRmlKdUNRcnUzb2hocUxkZmdland2NVEzY0V3T0g3akFuM2M0dzF4T3dxWEZVYlBMN1NxSTQ4cUR3VndWRV94eThxV3dzRjhzNzNIdnpKeUZJQ2JmZjBFSXZuU0E0LVh6cVp3dGc4RE9RVF9JWXVhbXZTQXJWREtrVkIzbzh2akFkUVJwNE8wcVlWLXd4UzFqWmM2c0p0bkk4dDZR?oc=5)).

Microsoft's own commentary on Africa put it bluntly: the continent cannot secure agentic AI with yesterday's security model ([Microsoft Source via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOUVlXdEJ1YWxoUXlxaExDSVdtZG1va05GUmt5Q0xQOEN4ZU1JaGlxcGNuVjdRb0NvdGR6T0gtcTNlUUJlbUl1STN0bldVZm1jR0hfRXktcEEtbTJNTlhfX2pvak96QTRDYmxzVGVjdUU5a09IQWJSbWNqbnNaZkc1VEhnTVZpSEtSOVJqdG54UGo1RWp5TzV5SGdIZkxiSmtsYVl2SkhSMHB4b29IZFQ1Tg?oc=5). that lands uncomfortably close to home given how fast the CBK report says Kenyan banks are adopting AI and APIs, a connection edition-5 drew explicitly. the identity and access controls most teams still run were designed for humans clicking buttons, not autonomous agents holding service principal credentials and acting on their own. if your team is wiring agents into anything touching production credentials, rotate those secrets and scope permissions tightly, because attackers are already treating agent identities as a soft target.

## the fight over where compute lives

every edition covered some version of the global data center backlash, and the picture got more layered as the week went on. South Africa joined a growing pushback against American-owned data centers over land, water, and power use ([Rest of World via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMidEFVX3lxTE1pUEN0VEhLQ2Z4MWdudzR0SnBpMVRhazFCOTJ3Y1V6VVctOUV5aXdqRjhGWmtkcnRyYTVWUkdEeG8wTEtqY2p4V3RnQzRGSlhSV2xrRFVUQTYwRDZYMlRqZW1nYV9TOXVJczNTbmNMbXhxSGZf?oc=5), covered in edition-1, edition-3, edition-4, and edition-5). Forbes offered a genuine counterpoint rather than a rebuttal: the same data center model people resist in the US could bring reliable power to rural Africa as a side effect of the buildout ([Forbes via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitAFBVV95cUxNVDRldUVsbUtXUko2ZE95SVN4emdMWVIyTl82WjNmeGt2NFZmWUpCLWVvdEl6cnRtMTVrV1RzSno2S1d5TUdYRUdoVnIxVmlGMV9uY0RpZ1lZTFo1N2hSMHR1WVF2MmtQVHJ5U2NZcTZfVGxaOXpMR2lkb0p1NG1hWTF2OTV0ZldSSkpDX094TVg2T3JWLUFzLUV4ZEo2VVJ0QVNRWDN4U2lDZ3RYd25TeXBLVk0?oc=5), covered in edition-3, edition-4, and edition-5). two headlines about the same infrastructure, different verdicts depending on who's negotiating the deal and who's paying the electricity bill.

meanwhile: Reuters reported AWS still can't restore access to a Bahrain and UAE cloud data zone after war damage ([Reuters via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizAFBVV95cUxPcGhFMHZVNGthdVl1bjYza3ZOWVpRY3ZMS1JmZkgxVnJTWGRhZFdFS3JfYk1nX2Y1Nzl3RlZDN21lWFA3b0xUU1E3UkdZR0hPQXNXdG5seEhsQ0hUR1gxVnZCWHFpTXdQdGhGQkFKTFd0cW80QUNBdnpXYzdOX25zZEJpN2lkOW1IWVlnc0RZNDk0cGgzSTlNYXFaMS1EcUJTM0ZUUjlhS0FLV0NrbGtsM3BKOVlFOWdFUi1sbGQtWmRqX3ZsODdPczRYRUw?oc=5), covered in edition-3 and edition-4). no amount of multi-availability-zone architecture protects you if an entire region goes dark for geopolitical reasons rather than a bad deploy.

on the buildout side, capital keeps arriving regardless of the pushback: former Global Switch executives launched a new AI and cloud infrastructure investment firm, NVIDIA and Google announced an alliance to speed up data center interconnects ([Mexico Business News via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiqAFBVV95cUxPVVdWYjd0M0g2QnRiTTBObGN5dW5kTnhQakNvem1iQzBGajE1VlJySGhsdGl0a1JiR2k3UTd3VnJ3ZGhnTHIxak8zMGhTbzk2S3JFV2pKU2J5Wjhic295UGJCN25Fdml3NThaU3hNRFhlbWxaSEdzYnkyd1M0SC1URFdFUG8yRTRWS2hpVGgtYnQ1RFFNM0lWdHdIMFY5Nk5FbXFwRFJaQWs?oc=5), edition-4 and edition-5), and Google even launched a tensor processing unit (TPU) satellite to test whether AI data centers could one day run in orbit ([Chosunbiz via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiekFVX3lxTFAxb1hkR0FZc0o2cmdacnlEOUtIQ0lLZlN3eU1zRk1WWHBYTWNaamxTaGN2Z2JYSHQ2eTBZaC1PcDAxSHF5S2tGWk45QzVfLVMzUTJXd1p1Vk1nUUs0MHdYTm03bUpsd3lxTURrcDBEYUEzYTFOVmtIZHB30gGOAUFVX3lxTFBhQk5TcXlkdDJ6R1VnN1l5UlRFa0FEMmtEcHN6RUp6UXZ4YmVhNDhYeGlIREQtWWJxbVlYdDBEZkRhbnJFUTZGeTE2UzdOT08wX2l5UGlVdzlKMGRKUWpzNzBRWTNfQU1sNmZjOUpYS3Y0azVWUTh4UDNRcWp5M2NpUnV4aU1UMWcyOTcydmc?oc=5), edition-5 only). land-based fights and orbital experiments, running at the same time, over the same underlying question: where does the compute physically live.

## open source doing the quiet, useful work

not everything this week was infrastructure and threat models. NVIDIA released Isaac ROS 5.0, GPU-accelerated packages built on the open source Robot Operating System (ROS), aimed at robots that perceive and reason in dynamic environments rather than follow scripted paths ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/isaac-ros-5-0-agentic-open-source-robotics/), covered in every edition this week). paired with that, Toronto startup smartARM used DINOv2, Meta's open source vision model, to build a bionic prosthetic arm that recognizes everyday objects from a handful of reference photos and picks the right grip automatically, no manual switching required, optionally pairing with Meta's AI glasses for extra context ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/canadian-start-up-smartarm-uses-ai-to-create-intuitive-bionic-prosthetics/), covered in edition-1, edition-2, edition-4, and edition-5). that's the kind of application that makes "open source vision model" mean something concrete: someone gets a working hand on the first try, not after weeks of retraining.

## and apple finally folds

last but not least, Apple used its annual event, the first led by new CEO John Ternus, to introduce the iPhone Duo, a passport-shaped foldable phone starting at $1,999 ([Reuters via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiygFBVV95cUxPbWlNdDREU0FXTWdNYTAyajJUNHR3NFVDd3dVVV92Y2dXSGpkZ1RKVGE3RVduaWtLX3piNElOQm1BWlV5RGFrNlJpSHZYQ2lHakU3T0NrVmVFNWhsVXJSazlEakQwZkN1blVSdEFZSWVnUmZZWFFmcm5KRUZ2TXFaY3ZJT0U3ckhVLWhvQ3lIalBZWXRSMzNORXV2YnBUT2dORVZFYWw1aHJWbl9MdFBpcGoyb2hOcG10VXVzZ0k0SzM4b0h5RnhMOTJB?oc=5), covered in every edition this week). Samsung and various Chinese OEMs have shipped foldables for years, so this is less a technical leap and more Apple validating a form factor once it's boring enough to trust. if you build mobile experiences, foldables change layout assumptions in ways flexbox alone won't fix, though whether that becomes a real platform concern or stays a niche premium tier is still open.

## the thread through all five editions

pull it together and you get one honest pattern: infrastructure is growing everywhere (more mobile money accounts, more fiber in Kenya, more compute and interconnects globally), but the security and governance models around that infrastructure aren't keeping pace, especially anywhere agentic AI touches production credentials. treat every autonomous agent your team ships like a junior engineer with root access: capable, occasionally reckless, worth auditing. and if your product depends on a single cloud region, this week's Bahrain outage is your annual reminder to plan for the entire region going dark, not just a single node.
