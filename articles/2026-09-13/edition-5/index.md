## Kenya's regulators are trying to keep up

the Communications Authority of Kenya (CA) has opened public consultation on new network equipment rules covering security, IPv6 (the newer internet addressing protocol meant to replace IPv4), AI, e-waste, accessibility, and performance standards ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/kenya-network-equipment-rules-consultation/)). if you build or import networking gear for the Kenyan market, this is worth reading now, before it becomes mandatory later.

meanwhile the shift from NEMIS (the National Education Management Information System) to its successor, KEMIS, is hitting real friction: learner records, school selection, and funding allocations are all colliding with the migration ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/the-nemis-to-kemis-dilemma/)). anyone who has migrated a government-scale legacy database knows this story before reading it: the data model changes underneath a live population, and the people who suffer are the ones the system is supposed to serve.

separately, the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) is shutting down its customs system, iCMS, for 12 hours on September 13 for maintenance, a reminder that even critical trade infrastructure still runs on scheduled downtime windows rather than zero-downtime deploys.

## when Claude became a local political tool

Anthropic's latest threat report documents how criminals, spies, and propagandists tried to weaponize its Claude model globally ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/anthopic-claude-threat-report-ai-misuse/)), and one case study hits close to home: a Kenyan operator reportedly used Claude to mass-produce political posts engineered to look like organic grassroots opinion ahead of the 2027 elections ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/kenya-ai-claude-political-propaganda/)). this is not a hypothetical "AI could be misused someday" story. it is a documented, dated incident. if you build anything that ingests social media as a signal (sentiment tools, trend dashboards, election monitoring), you now have to assume a meaningful chunk of your input data is synthetic astroturfing, and design your pipelines to detect that instead of trusting volume as a proxy for authenticity.

## your smart TV and your passwords are also in play

LG smart TVs are reportedly scanning home networks and logging device data even when the TV is switched "off," according to an investigation ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/lg-smart-tvs-network-scanning-privacy/)). if you run a home lab or self-host services, put your smart TVs on an isolated VLAN or guest network. treat them as untrusted devices, because apparently they are treating your network the same way.

on a brighter note, Android now supports secure password and passkey transfer directly between supported password managers, without forcing users to export unencrypted files first ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/android-password-manager-transfer/)). that unencrypted export step has been a quiet vulnerability in password manager migrations for years; closing it is a genuinely good, boring security improvement.

## agentic defense enters the arena

at CrowdStrike's Fal.Con 2026 conference, NVIDIA's Jensen Huang and CrowdStrike's George Kurtz announced SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system built to counter automated, AI-driven attacks with automated, AI-driven defense ([NVIDIA](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-crowdstrike-fal-con-2026/)). the framing from Huang was blunt: attacks are automated now, so defense has to be too. worth watching, though "our AI defends against their AI" is also exactly the kind of arms race that tends to escalate costs for everyone, including you, the engineer who eventually has to operate these systems in production.
