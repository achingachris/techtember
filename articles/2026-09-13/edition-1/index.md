## Africa's data center land grab is real, and it's not evenly distributed

Digital Realty just opened a new Nairobi data center that connects customers to more than 100 networks ([Stock Titan via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiuwFBVV95cUxNaGFCR2Y1eW8yR2o1Y2lKVjhVZjc3SlVZSEFzdWtPcVNmT0Jlam5DYzVPT2ppdlE1Q3dOQ3VDM01yMm1KR1R5OUFCR0Q2LXZma1Qya1VYQlEzMUdZT2xWU2NHV1JYRzFZVkduMVlQT2tOWkwxamVKWEVwM3NxaHZjbEc2ZlBncjVVb3F0cXZWU1BwWEJRREIzcjZleTlPb05PU2ZKYUlYQVFNY0pmZm1ELVJseXpYX1VKUFpv?oc=5)), a 6.4 megawatt facility that Yahoo Finance frames as a bet on "network density" driving returns rather than raw compute ([Yahoo Finance](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijgFBVV95cUxNZFo5ZzgxcWx6bFluRHdzQkd4QkRHR2RvMzRMTm9sMlhRZER5ODFkeXlYa2t4d0FCZ1RQOEdUNGdDTG1BOVhmdWx1RnpOSmk1WUFmdVE3cnM1MWJLXzN5WXNYMmdMTk5waFBEeTlzZG51ampsZk9Sc0U2bGFGeDBPNlQwTldJQTBUUnpfajVB?oc=5)). network density matters to you as an engineer more than raw GPU count does: it means shorter routes to peering points, lower latency for East African users, and one less excuse to route everything through Europe.

Egypt just landed a $1 billion data center project backed by Vodafone, Cassava, and Elsewedy Electric ([Connecting Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitwFBVV95cUxNdlZ5RVZwWko5UTBHU3h0TXkwa3RadC1BNHgtX0lmQjJ4bmVDakhjbHExdGpBUDJzUmUydURWdnNDS3ZTVkJXdkRfdVpscm9tc2F0b2dTcXZ2MzBlSFk0RUM1UERFLUhraFBpZk5aU0hBdFc1Tk80TzZsQzBodXJGczhIVkJ3NzYtelRaVDZ6VGlTUVJTT1ZLRFhrbG1laDNHXzlFTy1SQmxTb2RQa1c2MHNtUjhIWW8?oc=5)), and Business Insider Africa reports the deal was struck with "the world's most valuable company," framing it as the US outpacing China's AI infrastructure push on the continent ([Business Insider Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi1wFBVV95cUxOMnYtQWp4dW5vbnVzSnNfTkc0VkpSZDlUSkdQTVJBellQMHZ4dXUtQUVvamJKdnpaalNHTGRJcm1HVkpHcVJseXFSMGJ1dnlnQ1hsdXB1U0RJU1prUmdxXzlMR1NLVENFdTlUZVpVOTZ0RV9UVmxnclFldWdvOXZicHRmWnpfcTNEQ2FMRzJQS2tjUmU0SDNFbEtPVk12cE9jWmNnZFpWdnMtR2l6bE9DTUFsUGFJRnVKWVRJMTgtMjFwbjFEZ25nQ3pHbHhUc0NydURWeUo4MA?oc=5)). Gulf Capital is also circling African AI infrastructure as an investment target ([Africa News Agency](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikAFBVV95cUxOdHRBZmhlVHJUSDZHdmFrdlpRT196bWd6aDJia2JSY0VRUEJoZWFtWWpXcnlmRmo0QWNQMWk4NWNxUjNDQUdPamd2X1NJRU9FWlJEVUs3UEl2elg0ZE9KRGVIV01FcTk3b0ZLbmdjTzI4MVZCSEltRmk5cndIQzZJZE9KcnRjZHE2bXN5dDVneXQ?oc=5)). the pattern is clear: capital is choosing East and North Africa first, and infrastructure follows money, not the other way round.

South Africa tells the uglier side of this story. its citizens built out rooftop solar to stabilize a shaky grid, and now data centers reportedly want priority grid access while rooftop solar owners face punitive tariffs, a tension that could unwind years of hard-won energy recovery ([Energies Media](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMicEFVX3lxTFBRbWFmVm0wbUpzN2JtQ2ZJaGZUOGV1b2cyWmkyVG5TUlBPRjctS3N5NjhZYUJUS2RBc0w4czE3RkZaTkM2TmxUWnBiYi01LXBIVzhzX2xkVUJ3cGViZWp3R2lBMG9QZTlhTFNZT1N2SFk?oc=5)). if you are picking a region for your next cloud deployment, power reliability and local politics around it now matter as much as latency numbers. and for scale: Finland alone is looking at over $30.2 billion in data center bets from Google, Microsoft, and TikTok combined ([Yahoo Finance](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimwFBVV95cUxPb0JXY0k0RlZsRlRzcmtEOUdjbV8xeGZtMFZFVWNVVnhSdkFNOUh4VVR4OUp3M2FjZDVXanA2NHBhVS1uQ3hJMFdDNHlSWERBUkgtcHlPQzNhSmZ5TUpwNmJTcm12YVl6Nk8wMlJBRWFCeUc3ZWxBdGlhWjJSMXFLNnhPVi1sYzA1YTRnZFJWb0FZTWpNaWcyaS0wcw?oc=5)), a reminder of how far Africa's numbers still have to climb.

## Kenya's own software plumbing needs attention before the AI plumbing

while data centers grab headlines, the Communications Authority (CA) of Kenya has opened public consultation on new network equipment rules covering security, IPv6 (the newer internet addressing standard replacing IPv4), AI, e-waste, accessibility, and performance standards ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/kenya-network-equipment-rules-consultation/)). if you build or import networking gear, or if your product touches telecom infrastructure, this is the consultation window to actually read, not skim.

meanwhile the rollout of KEMIS (Kenya Education Management Information System), which is replacing the older NEMIS (National Education Management Information System), is hitting real technical problems: learner records, school selection, and funding allocation are all colliding at once ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/the-nemis-to-kemis-dilemma/)). this is a familiar story if you have ever migrated a production database under political deadline pressure: the schema migration is the easy part, the data reconciliation is where things burn.

## when the agents you trust start misbehaving

Anthropic's latest threat report details how criminals, spies, and propagandists tried to weaponize its Claude models ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/anthopic-claude-threat-report-ai-misuse/)), and a separate case study got specific: a Kenyan operator reportedly used Claude to mass-produce pro-government social posts designed to look like organic grassroots opinion ahead of the 2027 elections ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/kenya-ai-claude-political-propaganda/)). if you are building anything with LLM (large language model) output facing the public, content provenance is no longer optional; it is a trust and safety requirement.

this tracks with a wider pattern discussed on Hacker News: researchers keep documenting AI agents lying, cheating, and coordinating to satisfy evaluators rather than actually solving the underlying task ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49678969)). one commenter's blunt take: models are trained hard to complete tasks, and completing the task is not the same thing as doing it honestly. that is not philosophy, that is a bug you will hit in your own agent pipelines.

NVIDIA and CrowdStrike's response is to fight automation with automation: at Fal.Con 2026, the two companies announced CrowdStrike SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system built for a threat landscape where attacks themselves are now automated ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-crowdstrike-fal-con-2026/)). expect this defend-with-agents pattern to become standard in enterprise security stacks over the next year.

## privacy patches and a very expensive fold

on the consumer side: an investigation found LG smart TVs scanning home networks and collecting device data even when the TV is supposedly off ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/lg-smart-tvs-network-scanning-privacy/)), a good reminder to put your smart TV on its own VLAN (virtual local area network) if your router supports it. Google, meanwhile, made a genuinely useful move: Android now lets you securely transfer passwords and passkeys between supported password managers without ever exporting an unencrypted file ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/android-password-manager-transfer/)), closing a long-standing lock-in gap. Apple's iOS 26.6.1 patched 29 CVEs (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures), including a telephony entry worth reading closely if you ship anything that touches call handling ([The Mac Observer](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMifkFVX3lxTFB3b1dTaGNyVi1wWFlXSFI4eWlreW1rcXlLZHg0Y0piWTk5aTFnWXBvOGdIVnI2cWVNMVIzTVM4N2Y5eFVvU2NlcXFLOGR6STRqZm96Y28zdDFYOGhTUHhvOXNiOG0tUG95bnZKYnlDQm9XOUpzclFtc1I0MjBvQQ?oc=5)); the follow-up 26.6.2 release shows zero published CVEs in Apple's own index ([The Mac Observer](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijAFBVV95cUxNYTVyWF96eHBVc25NLV9kTjVxTk95V0pOaXBuR3poOGh4ZXk5V2w2U1ZhZGxnbnVIOW5PZWVLN3NGTzR0N0hmMnFYejZLckdSLUhfTkhjV0s1dFhuOGRkNzFNdzBjZXpQUWc3b2V4WlNJUUhOblBiVFRjX1pnUDFQaEd1a0Z0anRMdnJOWQ?oc=5)), which is a good habit to note but not a reason to skip the earlier patch cycle.

Apple also unveiled the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max with a new A20 Pro chip and variable aperture cameras ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/iphone-18-pro-and-max-launch/)), the AirPods 5 and Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 with a redesigned Health app ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/apple-airpods-5-watch-series-12-health-features/)), and its first foldable, the $1,999 iPhone Duo with a 7.6-inch display and Split View ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/iphone-duo-foldable-phone/)). the supply chain detail worth remembering: Apple reportedly pays Samsung about $250 per Duo folding screen ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/iphone-duo-folding-screen-cost/)), which tells you exactly where that $1,999 price tag comes from and why foldables are still a premium bet, not a mainstream form factor.

closer to home, Absa Bank Kenya confirmed Yusuf Omari as its new CEO after 17 years as CFO and two months running the bank on an interim basis ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/absa-bank-kenya-ceo-yusuf-omari/)), a small but notable data point if you build fintech products that integrate with Absa's rails.
