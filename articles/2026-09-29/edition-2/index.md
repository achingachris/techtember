this edition has two stories running in parallel. one is about kenya's digital plumbing finally catching up with its ambitions. the other is about the global infrastructure holding up that plumbing, and how it's becoming a bigger target than most of us realized. read them together and you get a decent picture of where "internet infrastructure" is headed in 2026.

## kenya's connectivity numbers finally look like progress

fixed internet subscriptions in Kenya grew 32% to 2.84 million, driven by fiber, satellite (read: Starlink), VoIP, and fixed wireless, even as traditional fixed-line phones keep disappearing ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/)). if you've been building anything that assumes flaky mobile data as the default connection for a Kenyan user, this is the number that should make you reconsider that assumption for at least a meaningful chunk of your user base.

that same growth is showing up on the money side. a Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) report found that Kenyan banks are increasingly adopting AI, APIs, cloud infrastructure, and digital lending, essentially turning themselves into fintechs from the inside ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/)). if you're building on top of banking rails in Kenya, expect more API surface area to integrate with, not less, and expect banks to start acting like competitors to the fintechs they used to just tolerate.

the enterprise software crowd showed up too. WSO2 held its first WSO2Con Africa in Nairobi, pulling together AI and API leaders for what the company called its biggest conference edition yet ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/25/wso2con-africa-2026-nairobi/)). Google also expanded Gemini access for Kenyan creators and students, though the coverage flags growing security and data protection concerns that come with wider AI rollout in a market still building out its regulatory muscle ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/google-gemini-in-kenya-creators-students/)). more access, more surface area, more questions about who's accountable when things go sideways. familiar pattern.

## the data centers holding this all up are getting pushback

here's the thing nobody tells you when you spin up a cloud resource: somewhere, a physical building full of servers is drinking power and water, and people who live near that building are starting to push back. church groups in the US asked Microsoft to commit 1% of its data center construction costs to community benefit funds, and Microsoft went quiet instead of answering ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/microsoft-goes-quiet-after-church-groups-ask-for-1-of-data-center-costs/)). South Africa is reportedly joining a broader global resistance movement against American-owned data centers on its soil ([Rest of World, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMidEFVX3lxTE1pUEN0VEhLQ2Z4MWdudzR0SnBpMVRhazFCOTJ3Y1V6VVctOUV5aXdqRjhGWmtkcnRyYTVWUkdEeG8wTEtqY2p4V3RnQzRGSlhSV2xrRFVUQTYwRDZYMlRqZW1nYV9TOXVJczNTbmNMbXhxSGZf?oc=5)). Forbes ran the counterpoint, arguing the same kind of facility that Americans hate could bring power infrastructure to rural Africa ([Forbes, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitAFBVV95cUxNVDRldUVsbUtXUko2ZE95SVN4emdMWVIyTl82WjNmeGt2NFZmWUpCLWVvdEl6cnRtMTVrV1RzSno2S1d5TUdYRUdoVnIxVmlGMV9uY0RpZ1lZTFo1N2hSMHR1WVF2MmtQVHJ5U2NZcTZfVGxaOXpMR2lkb0p1NG1hWTF2OTV0ZldSSkpDX094TVg2T3JWLUFzLUV4ZEo2VVJ0QVNRWDN4U2lDZ3RYd25TeXBLVk0?oc=5)), and musician EarthGang publicly warned about the harm these facilities can cause in African communities ([Connecting Africa, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirAFBVV95cUxNYW5vYVBTWnd5UU1jUnQ3Nzdya3pjZWtlWmJKYW1WNkdxbi1TZGo3WlNoOTJ0NDdyUUhsNTY3MF9FWGZ3Y0otWExGSEo5OVREMk83NVRLYzBIR05wdGdFMXhDeC1kTnlXZmZreUR6bFJnVFY4TW40VU94Sk9YWlRrNmEwLUVIQlJfTXJXamd6TEtRTFg2d2JwRnkwemdlVzBOWVZjdWlKVldyTDNt?oc=5)). no consensus here, just a genuine tension between "we need the compute" and "we didn't sign up to live next to it."

meanwhile, Samsung put $1 billion into KKR's Helix data center platform, explicitly to build out AI infrastructure ([Korea IT Times](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMicEFVX3lxTFBaSElWX2wwbmZDdkJjb3FRMXlnamxMbEZFOUJxUHV0Um1iSVZDUlNUbkdCY2xsVnViNGNxWXV6WHlfQmY4ZTN4WEw0VUNOM1JpaE9BOUU1c1c5aXA2bmdmclluOUxNc3FGZDRRUG52OTg?oc=5); [Data Center Dynamics](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiogFBVV95cUxNQW95NVUxT1oxSXdxdWdQYTN3b3NJR0xHRzg3SHVZT0l6amE5SE40elJIS2dPZ3dhZzJRRjJqMEhXb2NpS3NNZ1VIa1ltMFFucVJrSkItVkpsNzRfc0NIWFRpdVpEdXNrVTFaZmRzLVZFWDVDaTBKMmRBY21oenJORjRKWkl2ZVRqSWg0Q24wOGs4RWF4VnlYRURXbWFFVFNlMmc?oc=5)), which tells you the money is still flowing into this sector even as the political friction around it grows. and infrastructure can fail in dramatic, non-abstract ways too: Reuters reported that AWS has been unable to restore access to a data zone covering Bahrain and the UAE after war-related damage ([Reuters, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizAFBVV95cUxPcGhFMHZVNGthdVl1bjYza3ZOWVpRY3ZMS1JmZkgxVnJTWGRhZFdFS3JfYk1nX2Y1Nzl3RlZDN21lWFA3b0xUU1E3UkdZR0hPQXNXdG5seEhsQ0hUR1gxVnZCWHFpTXdQdGhGQkFKTFd0cW80QUNBdnpXYzdOX25zZEJpN2lkOW1IWVlnc0RZNDk0cGgzSTlNYXFaMS1EcUJTM0ZUUjlhS0FLV0NrbGtsM3BKOVlFOWdFUi1sbGQtWmRqX3ZsODdPczRYRUw?oc=5)). if your product depends on a specific cloud region being permanently available, this is a reminder that "the cloud" is still buildings, cables, and geopolitics.

## the cloud you rent can now be stolen from under you

the security story of the week is LLM-jacking: attackers steal cloud credentials, hijack the AI computing resources tied to those accounts, and leave the actual account owner holding the bill ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/28/llm-jacking-cloud-ai-cybercrime/)). this is the AI era's version of cryptojacking, and if you're a Kenyan startup running inference workloads on a shared cloud account with loose IAM (identity and access management) policies, this is exactly the kind of bill you don't want to explain to your finance team.

Microsoft separately documented Storm-3168, a campaign using compromised service principals (the credentials automated systems use to act on your behalf in the cloud) to run agentic-driven attacks ([Microsoft, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizwFBVV95cUxNM092d3lCWFRTMmMxZ09DODM4Z3hxWUNzSV93OVFoYUU3T2tYTUxudnMyc2MzR1R0QU1QSEI2ZzJQRjNfaUNVOUVRUU9LLTNSalE4SkEyM0FWcHN0MDlJb2dwcU8zNUhtbDYySjJLU3VYaVBvb1lLQUI2T2huNTduTkRiQjRYcWJ4WG9HNGpZSFNrbW5iSDVLSVdwUXpmRUdQTWt4RHVfUm5RQ1NJaDBKYWZjMDdNY0RGREpSeWZUeVlsVmd0eVJXZS1PM2xqWkU?oc=5)). the pattern across both stories is the same: attackers aren't necessarily breaking new AI models, they're breaking the identity and access layer around them, then letting the AI infrastructure do expensive work on their behalf.

Nvidia responded with a software platform meant to stop AI agents from misbehaving and to put security controls into both software and hardware for agent workloads ([CNBC Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiogFBVV95cUxQRWhmVDJGODhTOUJGeFdXajVQNHVRSEdfMUM3bWtmS1FUUXlUdzFpR2dRbFVlTUtDMDRpbEhaVExFRDZRSHhLaFFkRWFhYjAwZUxrYm9qVTFyc1B2R25MTWZlRThsRGpZbE90amlOUFpERzJQR05UeGtoVlppb1lrUUltdkl2ZDZYN0otazdVZ29rcVJGMU55OVVfc2FyRjZtRlE?oc=5); [engineering.com](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijwFBVV95cUxOZTBQTG85RTJ5X1hUYmJUWWVBLVZHLVJ6SmxDd3dmT3ZFc3dVXzV6a0NfSTJEZmNxbTJUQWJXLXBIdzNTWnhZcEpZYlBBWFJ3Z2NPd193Z2xzQjRya042cmhKQjc4UDF6QTFrbVhZTUdnT1A2NzViNE90aDZ4WUZ3MC1YWk5nbi16OEZDN2JMQQ?oc=5); [Tech Edition](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikwFBVV95cUxObnZRd0pfUTltbmEydUVLR1c3ejZqRXRNdTR1OXhOSHBsVlIzNEFZMFliR3Q4ZkhyWFMyaDFmUFNvelpjdVhLSG0tNjVTdDVybFRvZTFiN1hzUE9BSlhXTU9pSElWVHdRWUpCQ1E3NUd0X2xIb096SUFKeDJOVjlQZndMemZYVlhDemswZ2lyVDYxeFE?oc=5)), and markets rewarded cybersecurity stocks like Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, and Zscaler on the back of Nvidia's move alongside the security scares ([Stocktwits](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMigwJBVV95cUxOLXhwMzA4d1d4c3RJTXdVUGxYQjhQV2xHUXh2SklkSXpGdmwxYnA5dDE3MTA4Ymh1RVpnQnpqQXZ4Mmk2LUxYaVdmb3N2NDlObFpNQ1NvdFBGYm1Fa3h2cDJVTVNESUk3M2JFWWQwRjNMYk92SUZOWV8tRHhybUNBeV9PUnlSb21pcm5jYmMtYXZCaFZNNkhWYmRDRGdGUm14dHdZajJPY3hoTktvTVJNVDlnMDhlMXQwUTc1djR6OXhmVU5Gb0Y4YWRWU0lDRTFvV1l3TEptSWFYX1RfeHBxeVByTF9iV2Y4R2M1cWtOMzdFcmdmZzVvcEtRV2pFTm9zd2ww?oc=5)). Microsoft's own security team argues, bluntly, that Africa cannot secure agentic AI (AI systems that take autonomous multi-step actions) with yesterday's security model ([Microsoft Source, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOUVlXdEJ1YWxoUXlxaExDSVdtZG1va05GUmt5Q0xQOEN4ZU1JaGlxcGNuVjdRb0NvdGR6T0gtcTNlUUJlbUl1STN0bldVZm1jR0hfRXktcEEtbTJNTlhfX2pvak96QTRDYmxzVGVjdUU5a09IQWJSbWNqbnNaZkc1VEhnTVZpSEtSOVJqdG54UGo1RWp5TzV5SGdIZkxiSmtsYVl2SkhSMHB4b29IZFQ1Tg?oc=5)). agreed, and if you're advising a fintech or bank in Kenya that's rushing toward the AI-driven digital lending trend mentioned earlier, this is the exact moment to slow down and audit your identity and access controls before you scale.
