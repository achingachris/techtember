## the fixed line is dead, long live fiber

Kenya's fixed internet subscriptions jumped 32% to 2.84 million connections, and the driver isn't the old copper phone line anymore. It's fiber, satellite (think Starlink), VoIP, and fixed wireless quietly taking over while landlines fade out, according to [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/). If you're building anything that assumes patchy mobile data as the default connection for Kenyan users, that assumption is aging fast. Faster, more stable home and office connections change what you can reasonably ship: bigger web bundles, real time video, cloud IDEs that don't choke on every request.

That infrastructure shift is happening alongside a software one. A Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) report found that local banks are "quietly turning into fintechs," leaning on APIs, cloud infrastructure, AI, and digital lending to compete with the M-PESA generation of financial products, per [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/). If you work in Django or Node backends touching payments in Kenya, expect more banks to show up with real API-first platforms instead of the SOAP-and-a-prayer integrations of a few years ago. WSO2 picked a good week to run its first WSO2Con Africa in Nairobi, gathering API and enterprise integration folks to talk about exactly this shift, as covered by [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/25/wso2con-africa-2026-nairobi/). Google also expanded Gemini access for Kenyan creators and students in the same window, per [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/google-gemini-in-kenya-creators-students/), which is a nice complement to better bandwidth: more people who can actually use AI tools without their connection timing out.

## your cloud bill has a new enemy: LLM-jacking

Here's the less fun part. Attackers are hijacking cloud credentials specifically to run large language model (LLM) workloads on someone else's dime, a technique now nicknamed "LLM-jacking," reported by [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/28/llm-jacking-cloud-ai-cybercrime/). The pattern is simple and depressingly familiar: steal or phish a cloud credential, spin up inference jobs against expensive AI models, and vanish before the victim notices the invoice. Microsoft's own threat intelligence team documented a related campaign, Storm-3168, involving agentic-driven cloud attacks that abuse compromised service principals rather than plain user credentials, according to [Microsoft](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizwFBVV95cUxNM092d3lCWFRTMmMxZ09DODM4Z3hxWUNzSV93OVFoYUU3T2tYTUxudnMyc2MzR1R0QU1QSEI2ZzJQRjNfaUNVOUVRUU9LLTNSalE4SkEyM0FWcHN0MDlJb2dwcU8zNUhtbDYySjJLU3VYaVBvb1lLQUI2T2huNTduTkRiQjRYcWJ4WG9HNGpZSFNrbW5iSDVLSVdwUXpmRUdQTWt4RHVfUm5RQ1NJaDBKYWZjMDdNY0RGREpSeWZUeVlsVmd0eVJXZS1PM2xqWkU?oc=5). Service principals are meant to be the boring, machine-to-machine credentials nobody thinks about. Attackers thought about them.

A Microsoft Source piece framed the stakes plainly for our region: Africa cannot secure agentic AI with yesterday's security model, arguing that traditional identity and access management wasn't built for autonomous agents making their own API calls at machine speed, per [Microsoft Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOUVlXdEJ1YWxoUXlxaExDSVdtZG1va05GUmt5Q0xQOEN4ZU1JaGlxcGNuVjdRb0NvdGR6T0gtcTNlUUJlbUl1STN0bldVZm1jR0hfRXktcEEtbTJNTlhfX2pvak96QTRDYmxzVGVjdUU5a09IQWJSbWNqbnNaZkc1VEhnTVZpSEtSOVJqdG54UGo1RWp5TzV5SGdIZkxiSmtsYVl2SkhSMHB4b29IZFQ1Tg?oc=5). If you're an engineer with any AI agent touching your infra (even a simple one that reads tickets and calls an internal API), rotate its credentials like you would a human's, scope its permissions tightly, and log every call it makes. Treat it as a junior developer with root access and a caffeine problem.

Nvidia responded to this exact category of risk with a new software platform meant to stop AI agents from misbehaving, covered by [CNBC Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiogFBVV95cUxQRWhmVDJGODhTOUJGeFdXajVQNHVRSEdfMUM3bWtmS1FUUXlUdzFpR2dRbFVlTUtDMDRpbEhaVExFRDZRSHhLaFFkRWFhYjAwZUxrYm9qVTFyc1B2R25MTWZlRThsRGpZbE90amlOUFpERzJQR05UeGtoVlppb1lrUUltdkl2ZDZYN0otazdVZ29rcVJGMU55OVVfc2FyRjZtRlE?oc=5), with additional detail from [Tech Edition](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikwFBVV95cUxObnZRd0pfUTltbmEydUVLR1c3ejZqRXRNdTR1OXhOSHBsVlIzNEFZMFliR3Q4ZkhyWFMyaDFmUFNvelpjdVhLSG0tNjVTdDVybFRvZTFiN1hzUE9BSlhXTU9pSElWVHdRWUpCQ1E3NUd0X2xIb096SUFKeDJOVjlQZndMemZYVlhDemswZ2lyVDYxeFE?oc=5) and [Engineering.com](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijwFBVV95cUxOZTBQTG85RTJ5X1hUYmJUWWVBLVZHLVJ6SmxDd3dmT3ZFc3dVXzV6a0NfSTJEZmNxbTJUQWJXLXBIdzNTWnhZcEpZYlBBWFJ3Z2NPd193Z2xzQjRya042cmhKQjc4UDF6QTFrbVhZTUdnT1A2NzViNE90aDZ4WUZ3MC1YWk5nbi16OEZDN2JMQQ?oc=5). The pattern is clear: 2026 is the year "AI agent identity" becomes its own security discipline, not an afterthought bolted onto normal IAM.

## why this matters if you write code

Put the pieces together and the picture is straightforward: Kenya's internet is getting faster and more reliable, banks are becoming API-driven fintechs, and both of those things mean more traffic and more integrations to secure. At the same time, AI agents (yours or an attacker's) are becoming the thing identity and access management forgot to plan for. If you ship anything with a cloud credential attached to an autonomous process this year, budget time for scoping it down, not just building the feature.
