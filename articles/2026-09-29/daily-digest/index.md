five editions ran today, and read back to back they argue with each other in the best way. one says fiber is winning in Kenya. another says your cloud bill might be funding a stranger's AI habit. a third says nobody wants a data center next door, except when they desperately do. here's the day, synthesized.

## kenya's connectivity numbers finally have a plot twist

every edition today opened with the same headline, and for good reason: fixed internet subscriptions in Kenya grew 32% (edition-3 puts it at 32.4%) to 2.84 million connections. fixed-line phones are basically dead; edition-3 put a number on it, domestic fixed voice traffic collapsed 89.4% year on year to just 6,085 subscriptions nationwide, with DSL down to 23 subscriptions total. rest in peace, indeed.

what replaced copper is fiber (up 29.7% to 1.57 million subscriptions), satellite (up 54.4%, with Starlink already at 1% market share and 27,616 subscriptions), VoIP, and fixed wireless, according to the Communications Authority data covered across [edition-1](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/) through [edition-5](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/), all citing [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/). Safaricom still leads fixed data with 36.1% share, but the tail is getting crowded.

if you're an engineer, stop assuming every Kenyan user is on flaky mobile data by default. it's still true for plenty of people. it's just not the safe universal assumption it used to be.

## banks quietly turned into fintechs while nobody was watching

the second running thread was the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) 2025 Bank Supervision Annual Report, which found 84% of commercial banks and 79% of microfinance banks have adopted APIs (application programming interfaces), and about half have adopted some form of AI, mostly for credit risk scoring, cybersecurity, and customer service, per edition-3's deep dive into [Techweez's coverage](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/). the uncomfortable detail: only 30% of those institutions have a formal AI strategy, and 93% of them want CBK to actually publish AI governance rules. that gap between "we deployed it" and "we have a policy for it" is exactly where incidents happen.

it's not a coincidence WSO2 chose Nairobi for its first WSO2Con Africa, run under the theme "Building the Agentic Enterprise" and pulling in government ICT officials, per [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/25/wso2con-africa-2026-nairobi/), a story every edition referenced today. Google also expanded Gemini access for Kenyan creators and students, per [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/google-gemini-in-kenya-creators-students/), though edition-2 and edition-5 both flagged growing security and data protection concerns as that access outpaces the regulatory guardrails around it.

## someone stole a cloud account just to run an AI model on it

if one story unified all five editions, it's this: attackers are stealing cloud credentials specifically to run large language model (LLM) inference workloads, a technique now called "LLM-jacking." edition-3 had the sharpest detail: Google's Mandiant team investigated a case where an exposed personal access token was used to spin up unauthorized GPU-heavy AI infrastructure, and the account owner got stuck with the bill, per [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/28/llm-jacking-cloud-ai-cybercrime/). every edition landed on the same advice: rotate leaked keys, prefer short-lived credentials with least-privilege scopes, set billing alerts and GPU quota limits, and keep dev, test, and production in separate accounts.

Microsoft's threat intelligence team separately documented "Storm-3168," agentic-driven cloud attacks abusing compromised service principals (machine-to-machine credentials that don't trigger MFA prompts or suspicious-login alerts the way human logins do), per [Microsoft](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizwFBVV95cUxNM092d3lCWFRTMmMxZ09DODM4Z3hxWUNzSV93OVFoYUU3T2tYTUxudnMyc2MzR1R0QU1QSEI2ZzJQRjNfaUNVOUVRUU9LLTNSalE4SkEyM0FWcHN0MDlJb2dwcU8zNUhtbDYySjJLU3VYaVBvb1lLQUI2T2huNTduTkRiQjRYcWJ4WG9HNGpZSFNrbW5iSDVLSVdwUXpmRUdQTWt4RHVfUm5RQ1NJaDBKYWZjMDdNY0RGREpSeWZUeVlsVmd0eVJXZS1PM2xqWkU?oc=5). Microsoft Source went further, arguing that Africa cannot secure agentic AI (AI systems taking autonomous multi-step actions) with yesterday's identity and access management model, per [Microsoft Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOUVlXdEJ1YWxoUXlxaExDSVdtZG1va05GUmt5Q0xQOEN4ZU1JaGlxcGNuVjdRb0NvdGR6T0gtcTNlUUJlbUl1STN0bldVZm1jR0hfRXktcEEtbTJNTlhfX2pvak96QTRDYmxzVGVjdUU5a09IQWJSbWNqbnNaZkc1VEhnTVZpSEtSOVJqdG54UGo1RWp5TzV5SGdIZkxiSmtsYVl2SkhSMHB4b29IZFQ1Tg?oc=5), a claim edition-1 and edition-2 both leaned on hard.

South Africa is reportedly joining a broader global resistance movement against American-owned data centers, per [Rest of World](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMidEFVX3lxTE1pUEN0VEhLQ2Z4MWdudzR0SnBpMVRhazFCOTJ3Y1V6VVctOUV5aXdqRjhGWmtkcnRyYTVWUkdEeG8wTEtqY2p4V3RnQzRGSlhSV2xrRFVUQTYwRDZYMlRqZW1nYV9TOXVJczNTbmNMbXhxSGZf?oc=5), and Grammy-nominated group EarthGang has publicly warned about the environmental and community harm these facilities can cause on the continent, per [Connecting Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirAFBVV95cUxNYW5vYVBTWnd5UU1jUnQ3Nzdya3pjZWtlWmJKYW1WNkdxbi1TZGo3WlNoOTJ0NDdyUUhsNTY3MF9FWGZ3Y0otWExGSEo5OVREMk83NVRLYzBIR05wdGdFMXhDeC1kTnlXZmZreUR6bFJnVFY4TW40VU94Sk9YWlRrNmEwLUVIQlJfTXJXamd6TEtRTFg2d2JwRnkwemdlVzBOWVZjdWlKVldyTDNt?oc=5).

## the honest read on today

strip away the AI-agent framing and the real story of today is infrastructure catching up with ambition, on three fronts at once: Kenya's internet finally has the bandwidth its apps deserve, banks are rebuilding themselves as API-first platforms faster than their governance policies can keep up, and the physical question of where compute lives, and who's angry about it, is becoming as important as the code running on top of it. none of that needs an AI agent to be interesting. it just needs you to notice that plumbing stories are usually more important than the shiny thing sitting on top of the pipes.
