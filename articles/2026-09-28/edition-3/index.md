technology news this week wasn't really about a single breakthrough. it was about who pays for infrastructure, and who gets stuck holding the bill when things go wrong. that theme runs through cloud security, data center politics, and Kenya's own connectivity numbers. there's a foldable iPhone in here too, because even infrastructure weeks need a hardware headline.

## the cloud bill nobody agreed to pay

Google is warning that attackers are stealing cloud credentials specifically to run AI models for free, a practice being called "LLM-jacking" (large language model jacking), where LLM refers to the large language models behind tools like ChatGPT or Gemini. the attackers get free inference. you get the invoice ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/28/llm-jacking-cloud-ai-cybercrime/)). if you've ever left an API key in a public repo, this is the scenario that should keep you up at night.

Microsoft is seeing a related pattern with a threat group it tracks as Storm-3168, which runs agentic-driven cloud attacks using compromised service principals, the identity objects Azure uses to let applications authenticate without a human logging in ([Microsoft via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizwFBVV95cUxNM092d3lCWFRTMmMxZ09DODM4Z3hxWUNzSV93OVFoYUU3T2tYTUxudnMyc2MzR1R0QU1QSEI2ZzJQRjNfaUNVOUVRUU9LLTNSalE4SkEyM0FWcHN0MDlJb2dwcU8zNUhtbDYySjJLU3VYaVBvb1lLQUI2T2huNTduTkRiQjRYcWJ4WG9HNGpZSFNrbW5iSDVLSVdwUXpmRUdQTWt4RHVfUm5RQ1NJaDBKYWZjMDdNY0RGREpSeWZUeVlsVmd0eVJXZS1PM2xqWkU?oc=5)). same idea as classic credential theft, just pointed at automated agents instead of a person clicking a link.

Nvidia's response is a software platform meant to stop AI agents from misbehaving, essentially guardrails for agent identity and behavior at the infrastructure level ([CNBC Africa via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiogFBVV95cUxQRWhmVDJGODhTOUJGeFdXajVQNHVRSEdfMUM3bWtmS1FUUXlUdzFpR2dRbFVlTUtDMDRpbEhaVExFRDZRSHhLaFFkRWFhYjAwZUxrYm9qVTFyc1B2R25MTWZlRThsRGpZbE90amlOUFpERzJQR05UeGtoVlppb1lrUUltdkl2ZDZYN0otazdVZ29rcVJGMU55OVVfc2FyRjZtRlE?oc=5), [Engineering.com via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijwFBVV95cUxOZTBQTG85RTJ5X1hUYmJUWWVBLVZHLVJ6SmxDd3dmT3ZFc3dVXzV6a0NfSTJEZmNxbTJUQWJXLXBIdzNTWnhZcEpZYlBBWFJ3Z2NPd193Z2xzQjRya042cmhKQjc4UDF6QTFrbVhZTUdnT1A2NzViNE90aDZ4WUZ3MC1YWk5nbi16OEZDN2JMQQ?oc=5)). worth watching if you're building anything with agentic workflows in production. identity and access management for machines is the new frontier, and it's messier than IAM (identity and access management) for humans ever was.

## data centers, wanted and unwanted

the physical side of "the cloud" is having its own reckoning. in the US, church groups have asked Microsoft for 1% of local data center costs to fund community benefits, and Microsoft has gone quiet in response, according to Ars Technica ([arstechnica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/microsoft-goes-quiet-after-church-groups-ask-for-1-of-data-center-costs/)). South Africa is now part of a broader global pushback against American-owned data centers, joining communities worried about water use, power draw, and who actually benefits locally ([Rest of World via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMidEFVX3lxTE1pUEN0VEhLQ2Z4MWdudzR0SnBpMVRhazFCOTJ3Y1V6VVctOUV5aXdqRjhGWmtkcnRyYTVWUkdEeG8wTEtqY2p4V3RnQzRGSlhSV2xrRFVUQTYwRDZYMlRqZW1nYV9TOXVJczNTbmNMbXhxSGZf?oc=5)).

Forbes flips the framing: the same kind of data center Americans are protesting could light up rural Africa, if the power and connectivity infrastructure gets built alongside it instead of walled off ([Forbes via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitAFBVV95cUxNVDRldUVsbUtXUko2ZE95SVN4emdMWVIyTl82WjNmeGt2NFZmWUpCLWVvdEl6cnRtMTVrV1RzSno2S1d5TUdYRUdoVnIxVmlGMV9uY0RpZ1lZTFo1N2hSMHR1WVF2MmtQVHJ5U2NZcTZfVGxaOXpMR2lkb0p1NG1hWTF2OTV0ZldSSkpDX094TVg2T3JWLUFzLUV4ZEo2VVJ0QVNRWDN4U2lDZ3RYd25TeXBLVk0?oc=5)). that's the optimistic version. the sobering version is that this infrastructure is fragile in ways engineers underestimate: Amazon's AWS (Amazon Web Services) still hasn't restored access to its Bahrain data zone and one UAE zone after war damage, per Reuters ([Reuters via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizAFBVV95cUxPcGhFMHZVNGthdVl1bjYza3ZOWVpRY3ZMS1JmZkgxVnJTWGRhZFdFS3JfYk1nX2Y1Nzl3RlZDN21lWFA3b0xUU1E3UkdZR0hPQXNXdG5seEhsQ0hUR1gxVnZCWHFpTXdQdGhGQkFKTFd0cW80QUNBdnpXYzdOX25zZEJpN2lkOW1IWVlnc0RZNDk0cGgzSTlNYXFaMS1EcUJTM0ZUUjlhS0FLV0NrbGtsM3BKOVlFOWdFUi1sbGQtWmRqX3ZsODdPczRYRUw?oc=5)). if your architecture assumes a region never goes down, it's worth reading the multi-region failover chapter again. meanwhile Nvidia and Google have launched an alliance specifically to speed up data center interconnects, which tells you where the real bottleneck in AI infrastructure sits right now: not compute, but the network between compute ([Mexico Business News via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiqAFBVV95cUxPVVdWYjd0M0g2QnRiTTBObGN5dW5kTnhQakNvem1iQzBGajE1VlJySGhsdGl0a1JiR2k3UTd3VnJ3ZGhnTHIxak8zMGhTbzk2S3JFV2pKU2J5Wjhic295UGJCN25Fdml3NThaU3hNRFhlbWxaSEdzYnkyd1M0SC1URFdFUG8yRTRWS2hpVGgtYnQ1RFFNM0lWdHdIMFY5Nk5FbXFwRFJaQWs?oc=5)).

## Kenya's quiet infrastructure boom

back home, the boring numbers are the interesting story. fixed internet subscriptions in Kenya surged 32% to 2.84 million, driven by fiber, Starlink, VoIP (voice over internet protocol), and fixed wireless, even as traditional fixed-line phones disappear ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/fixed-line-internet-connections-kenya/)). that's the kind of connectivity growth that makes remote work and cloud-hosted apps viable outside Nairobi's core, not just possible in theory.

the Central Bank of Kenya's own report shows local banks quietly turning into fintechs, adopting AI, APIs (application programming interfaces), cloud infrastructure, and digital lending at a pace that used to be reserved for challenger banks ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/)). if you're building fintech integrations in Kenya, expect more banks exposing APIs, not fewer. WSO2Con Africa held its first Nairobi edition, pulling in API and enterprise integration leaders from across the continent ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/25/wso2con-africa-2026-nairobi/)), and Google is expanding Gemini for Kenyan creators and students, though techweez flags growing security and data protection concerns that come with wider AI rollout in a market still building out its data protection enforcement ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/google-gemini-in-kenya-creators-students/)). that concern isn't hypothetical, given the LLM-jacking story above. more AI access means more credentials worth stealing.

## hardware and robotics, briefly

Apple finally joined the foldable phone race with a $1,999 passport-shaped iPhone Duo, according to Reuters and Mashable's event recap ([Reuters via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiygFBVV95cUxPbWlNdDREU0FXTWdNYTAyajJUNHR3NFVDd3dVVV92Y2dXSGpkZ1RKVGE3RVduaWtLX3piNElOQm1BWlV5RGFrNlJpSHZYQ2lHakU3T0NrVmVFNWhsVXJSazlEakQwZkN1blVSdEFZSWVnUmZZWFFmcm5KRUZ2TXFaY3ZJT0U3ckhVLWhvQ3lIalBZWXRSMzNORXV2YnBUT2dORVZFYWw1aHJWbl9MdFBpcGoyb2hOcG10VXVzZ0k0SzM4b0h5RnhMOTJB?oc=5), [Mashable via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMigwFBVV95cUxNQVVuY3B2d0hxVnV6SjhYd283UHpPelV6RVBCcXdJZk1DNDRocjNwa1BqU3JmSlhvZ3I4WEhPUUVQM3E0YTZxcDkyQjgtY1hiX3dzTVJKUGlCWGNWN09kSlZNSXdFaktWYllSbm1fb3ZDVDh6dkNuLXEwS25GZDhSV04wcw?oc=5)). a passport is a strange design reference, but it fits: expensive, folds, and you hope you never need to replace it before its time.

on the robotics side, NVIDIA shipped Isaac ROS 5.0, a set of GPU-accelerated packages built on ROS (robot operating system), the open framework from Open Robotics that most robotics engineers already build on ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/isaac-ros-5-0-agentic-open-source-robotics/)). it's aimed at agentic, open source robotics development, meaning more of the perception and reasoning stack now ships as reusable, GPU-backed components instead of custom code per robot. if you've been putting off looking at ROS, this release is a reasonable excuse to start.

the pattern across all of this: infrastructure is getting more powerful and more contested at the same time. more compute, more connectivity, more APIs exposed, and more attackers looking for the gaps in between. build accordingly.
