## AI labs kept overselling their homework, and one incident hit close to home

two AI stories ran through nearly every edition, and both are about the gap between a claim and its evidence. OpenAI said its systems helped crack part of the 90-year-old Navier-Stokes problem in fluid dynamics. mathematicians pushed back hard on the training data and the transparency of the proof, a point every edition from 1 through 5 raised ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/14/openai-navier-stokes-proof/)). treat "AI solved a famous unsolved problem" the way you'd treat an unreviewed pull request: interesting, unverified, don't merge to production opinions yet.

the more consequential story was Anthropic's own threat report on Claude misuse (edition-1, edition-2, [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/anthopic-claude-threat-report-ai-misuse/)), and specifically the case where a Kenyan operator allegedly used Claude to mass-produce political posts designed to look like organic grassroots opinion ahead of the 2027 elections, covered in every edition this week ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/kenya-ai-claude-political-propaganda/)). this isn't a hypothetical threat model anymore. if you build anything with a large language model (LLM) touching public discourse in Kenya, content moderation and provenance detection are part of the product from day one, not a feature you bolt on after launch.
