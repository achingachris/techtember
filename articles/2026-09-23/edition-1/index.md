this edition had a bit of everything: security incidents you should actually care about, infrastructure news that explains why your cloud bill keeps climbing, and a couple of stories from Kenya that show fintech is quietly eating the banking sector from the inside. let's go through it.

## security: patch first, read later

two zero-day stories landed close together, and both matter if you ship consumer software.

first, [Google's Pixel modem has a zero-day (CVE-2026-58704) that's already being exploited in targeted attacks](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTE9OUEpxQ2dGT3FmdndFeFNTWWVEajdZQzIxQ05ic1h6aFlJZDh1MnF1UE94ZklpRVA4Nl81UXZWSmR4ekt0MmpLLWVtU1RIUlBwN0JXdUpBWUNiZmNfMFhyaEI5RGU2NHpuWjUxWnlvbTk1WW11X1hDcg?oc=5). modem firmware bugs are nasty because they sit below the operating system's usual sandboxing, so if you're managing fleets of Pixel devices (support desks, this means you), get the patch out.

second, [Meta's Muse AI assistant reportedly has a zero-day that lets attackers take over a Mac](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwAFBVV95cUxOS3o1M3ZVdUJmM2t6R2RhSWVHaFpwdnVKLWpVbDJpVlZSekZDMk5fV1VseWRpeFhKOFRadlUtVVNkUEduSE1jTThKLUZ3Nm5mdHNycGVPRm42aGNzTFI3X0VSMGJPZFJMX09ienJCRjJkdG5RNDh4MjVwZFlJQUNfMG9GNVkzbDRxekRmbXlHd0wxcnZ3RkRKUGVRQ2lHRmZmOWgwX2ZOdWdqZENVX1Jzc2RrSFBTOGtUZEFfSUhoOFY?oc=5), with the same finding echoed by [Malwarebytes](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivwFBVV95cUxPU1N2S1ZSUFRHdEJ4bnRuWDlZdlc5T3ZKSkJtZ2NSUlp0Q21rbk5LOGhjZzlLSkdld3lWQjBReF8yczZLeG92Z0dDUGY1U0J5TEVkdldsTlJGV25mRms0VlZfbGZ5cTkxMW0yNFF0SDl4eHdRNWVabHBZenJ1Y0Nrd0N2YVVBUTdEUlR0dXlBajRXSmZPLUNFZktpZGVqSEhKUU1RTTFRWGh5R2wtVnJobXhsbTJYdG8yb2NKdk44TQ?oc=5). the pattern is a familiar one: AI assistants get shipped with broad system permissions (file access, app control) because that's what makes them useful, and every one of those permissions is a new attack surface. if you're building an agent with local system access, treat its permission scope like you'd treat a production API key: least privilege, audited, revocable.

and if you needed a reminder that large centralized databases are a liability, not just an asset: hackers claim [they've obtained data on all FBI employees](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49805278). the Hacker News thread is worth reading for the "assume every internet-connected machine is semi-public" take, which is depressing but not wrong.

## the grid finally shows up in the AI conversation

data centers have been the quiet cost center behind every AI headline, and this edition is the first time i've seen the electricity grid treated as a first-class engineering constraint instead of an afterthought. [Emerald AI, Google, and NVIDIA launched the AI Energy Management Alliance (AEMA)](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-energy-management-alliance/), a coalition aimed at building data centers that can dynamically flex their power draw instead of just demanding a fixed slab of capacity 24/7. [Quartz's writeup](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMifkFVX3lxTE9KUmcyTy1naGQ5WDE3Qm04T3NqSHdKRHBSOXhZbW1ybGtiX2h4WmFjT2FyQkN6NkwtSkZ6VlpGWDlkemtzSmxlWkZ6ZXhYRnRuOWRabGZQM0dEbXV2TFpMaDdVU3h6OVo5TVlidnlONC1oRjJEdjZ5ZTlObGVQUQ?oc=5) frames it as the industry admitting that raw compute scaling was going to hit a wall made of transformers and transmission lines, not GPUs.

## Kenya: fintech is winning quietly

three Kenyan stories this edition point at the same trend: banks are turning into software companies whether they like it or not. [a CBK (Central Bank of Kenya) report shows Kenyan banks are quietly turning into fintechs](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/) by adopting AI, APIs, cloud, and digital lending, which tracks with what i've seen building integrations for local banks: nobody wants to be "just a bank" anymore. that shift is backed by real numbers: [mobile money subscriptions grew 13.2% to 54 million accounts by June 2026, with Safaricom's M-PESA holding an 88.8% market share](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/mobile-money-subscriptions-reach-54-million/). and [Google is expanding Gemini access for Kenyan creators and students](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/google-gemini-in-kenya-creators-students/), which is good for adoption but also raises the same data protection questions we should be asking about any AI tool aimed at students: where does the data go, and who governs it.

on the enterprise side, [WSO2 launched Agent Manager to tackle AI agent sprawl](https://techweez.com/2026/09/17/wso2-agent-manager/), giving companies one place to monitor, govern, and secure AI agents across models and frameworks. if you've deployed more than three internal AI agents already and lost track of what each one can touch, this category of tooling exists specifically for you.

## hardware and open source worth a look

on the robotics side, [NVIDIA released Isaac ROS 5.0, a set of GPU-accelerated packages built on the open source ROS (Robot Operating System) framework](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/isaac-ros-5-0-agentic-open-source-robotics/), aimed at robots that need to perceive and reason in real time rather than run pre-scripted routines.

a more human story: Toronto startup smartARM is [using Meta's open source DINOv2 vision model to build a bionic arm that recognizes everyday objects from just a few reference photos and automatically picks the right grip](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/canadian-start-up-smartarm-uses-ai-to-create-intuitive-bionic-prosthetics/). the interesting engineering detail is that it works without prior training on the specific object, it's reading visual features directly, and users can optionally pair it with Meta AI glasses for extra context. this is the kind of AI application that doesn't need a hype cycle to justify itself.

and if you want a palate cleanser: someone [ported Visual FoxPro (dead since 2007) to a new Rust runtime that compiles to WebAssembly](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49808023), validated against the real vfp9.exe binary, with lambdas, JSON, and an HTTP server bolted on. it's a reminder that "legacy" software often just means "software someone depends on and nobody wants to rewrite," and sometimes the sane move is a compatible runtime, not a rewrite.
