## Kenya's money rails are becoming software rails

Start with the numbers. Kenya's mobile money subscriptions grew 13.2% to 54 million accounts by June 2026, with Safaricom's M-Pesa still holding an 88.8% market share ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/mobile-money-subscriptions-reach-54-million/)). That is not a small platform anymore; it is national infrastructure, and it is being treated like one. A new Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) report found that Kenyan banks are quietly turning into fintechs themselves, leaning on AI, APIs, cloud technology, and digital lending to compete ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/)). Pair that with a proposed payment bill that would force banks and M-Pesa to open up their data ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/22/cbk-banking-fintech-report-2025/)), and you get the shape of where this is headed: open banking, API-first finance, in a market where the incumbent already has near-monopoly distribution.

If you build fintech products in Kenya, this is your roadmap. Banks adopting AI and APIs means more partners to integrate with, but also more surface area to secure. Which brings me to the part of this week's crawl that should worry you more than it excites you.

## Agentic AI's security reality check

WSO2 is telling Kenyan businesses to get their systems ready for AI agents ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/18/mobile-money-subscriptions-reach-54-million/)), and a companion piece argues Africa cannot secure agentic AI with yesterday's security model (via [Microsoft Source, aggregated on Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOUVlXdEJ1YWxoUXlxaExDSVdtZG1va05GUmt5Q0xQOEN4ZU1JaGlxcGNuVjdRb0NvdGR6T0gtcTNlUUJlbUl1STN0bldVZm1jR0hfRXktcEEtbTJNTlhfX2pvak96QTRDYmxzVGVjdUU5a09IQWJSbWNqbnNaZkc1VEhnTVZpSEtSOVJqdG54UGo1RWp5TzV5SGdIZkxiSmtsYVl2SkhSMHB4b29IZFQ1Tg?oc=5)). That is a reasonable warning to make in the abstract. It stopped being abstract this week: Meta's Muse AI assistant reportedly has a zero-day vulnerability that can turn it into a Mac backdoor ([Malwarebytes, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivwFBVV95cUxPU1N2S1ZSUFRHdEJ4bnRuWDlZdlc5T3ZKSkJtZ2NSUlp0Q21rbk5LOGhjZzlLSkdld3lWQjBReF8yczZLeG92Z0dDUGY1U0J5TEVkdldsTlJGV25mRms0VlZfbGZ5cTkxMW0yNFF0SDl4eHdRNWVabHBZenJ1Y0Nrd0N2YVVBUTdEUlR0dXlBajRXSmZPLUNFZktpZGVqSEhKUU1RTTFRWGh5R2wtVnJobXhsbTJYdG8yb2NKdk44TQ?oc=5)), and separately, Amazon has blocked the same Muse assistant in a standoff over agentic shopping ([GeekWire, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiqgFBVV95cUxNN1NIM3JxN1RPdlktMDMwTmlZcnk5MFV4b1loLVBvc3pfUElYT3ZhQ3VLZmVod3ZxeDBVTXNkWkhybWJTZE05YzdkSWpOMHpwOVlYbWJYNndSSWRkRlFQSmNsM0F2WWgzZTFLNjFuMHNhY2d3akstanhNRTlOSEZvQVV2alFiTUE4MnBHRlJjdy12RXhwclJXVTFTZWV2R1ZrUHhUdlZuZmVtdw?oc=5)).

Read those two together. An "agent" acting on your behalf, with your login sessions and your shopping cart, is a new API surface that platform owners like Amazon do not trust yet, and one that security researchers are already finding holes in. Kenyan businesses being told to prep for agentic AI is good advice, and "prep" should mean threat modeling agent permissions the same way you'd threat model a third-party OAuth integration, rather than buying a chatbot license and calling it done. If you are wiring an AI agent into anything that touches money, moderate its API scope like you would a compromised credential from day one, because someone will eventually treat it as one.

## Physical AI: robots and prosthetics that don't need a training manual

Not everything in AI is a security headache. NVIDIA released Isaac ROS 5.0, a set of GPU-accelerated packages built on the open source ROS (Robot Operating System) framework, aimed at robots that can perceive, reason, and act in dynamic environments ([NVIDIA](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/isaac-ros-5-0-agentic-open-source-robotics/)). On the inference side, NVIDIA's TensorRT Edge-LLM reportedly completed the MLPerf edge agentic benchmark 6.4 times faster on Jetson AGX Thor hardware ([NVIDIA Developer, via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMixgFBVV95cUxNRHI0WHhmMlRfRGN5ZGNSclJiUlJjUFBCbTFxeHQ2SFBubDhLUEZnaW4tNFBIVlZYeElrYWZVVW9tS1RhY0hTWTlvcHY0ajAzREJRMjZDVFNYNzhxZXdMZ3JvUzNYNmlOMV92Z3hRZGlfbHh0UVNPNDhtaUFvNldPcWQxanNoR2h1VWZvMk9KVDg4RmtEQzZ0dFpWMVZibmdHSWFfMmFianliSG1RcW54Zlg5RTFmMURjNVdHVlA4VG9uMU9helE?oc=5)), which matters if you are budgeting for on-device inference rather than round-tripping to a cloud API.

The most compelling application of this stack this week is not a robot at all. Toronto startup smartARM is building a vision-first bionic arm that uses a camera in the palm and Meta's open source DINOv2 vision model to recognize everyday objects from just a few reference photos, then automatically pick the right grip, no manual switching, no retraining ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/canadian-start-up-smartarm-uses-ai-to-create-intuitive-bionic-prosthetics/)). It optionally pairs with Meta AI glasses for extra egocentric context. This is the version of "AI hardware" worth paying attention to: narrow, well-scoped, and solving a genuinely hard human problem (grip selection) instead of bolting a chatbot onto a toaster.
