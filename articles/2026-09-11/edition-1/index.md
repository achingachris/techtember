## agentic AI needs agentic defense, and attackers already know it

at Fal.Con 2026, NVIDIA and CrowdStrike announced SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system built by CrowdStrike's Cyber unit, with NVIDIA's Jensen Huang framing it plainly: attacks are automated now, so defense has to be too ([NVIDIA and CrowdStrike Strengthen Agentic Cybersecurity Frontier](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-crowdstrike-fal-con-2026/)). that's not a marketing line you can ignore if you're shipping anything with an LLM in the loop. autonomous agents that can chain tool calls are also autonomous attack surfaces.

Anthropic's latest threat report backs that up with specifics: criminals, state-linked actors, propagandists, and even rival AI labs have all tried to weaponize Claude ([Anthropic Reveals How Criminals Tried to Weaponize Claude](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/anthopic-claude-threat-report-ai-misuse/)). if you're integrating any foundation model into a product, treat prompt injection and misuse the same way you'd treat SQL injection: as a default assumption, not an edge case.

## kenya's regulators are trying to catch up, unevenly

three stories this week show Kenya's institutions scrambling to keep pace with the tech they're supposed to govern. the Communications Authority (CA) opened public consultation on new network equipment rules covering security, IPv6, AI, e-waste, accessibility, and performance standards ([CA Opens Public Consultation on New Network Equipment Rules](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/kenya-network-equipment-rules-consultation/)). if you import or spec networking gear for a Kenyan deployment, this consultation window is your chance to flag issues before they become mandatory compliance headaches.

separately, Kenya launched a 140-page guide to help prosecutors investigate cybercrime and preserve digital evidence ([Kenya Launches New 140-Page Guide to Help Fight Cybercrime](https://techweez.com/2026/09/08/kenya-cybercrime-guide-launch/)). that's good news for incident response credibility, but it also means your logging and evidence-preservation practices now have a formal standard they'll be measured against.

meanwhile, the migration from NEMIS to KEMIS (Kenya's national school data system overhaul) is hitting real technical problems, with learner records and Grade 10 selection caught in the crossfire ([The NEMIS to KEMIS Dilemma](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/the-nemis-to-kemis-dilemma/)). it's a useful, sobering case study: data migrations at national scale fail in the same boring ways your database migrations do (schema mismatches, missing validation, insufficient rollback plans), just with far higher stakes.

## the boring security wins that actually matter

Google now lets Android users securely transfer passwords and passkeys between supported password managers without exporting unencrypted files ([Google Makes It Easier to Switch Password Managers on Android](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/android-password-manager-transfer/)). small feature, big deal: unencrypted password exports have been a quiet vulnerability for years, and removing that step is the kind of unglamorous fix that prevents real breaches.

On the flip side, an investigation found LG smart TVs scanning home networks and collecting device data even when the TV is supposedly off ([LG TVs Reportedly Scan Your Network and Track What You Watch](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/lg-smart-tvs-network-scanning-privacy/)). if you're building anything that touches a home network (IoT, smart home integrations, local device discovery), assume other devices on that network are watching too, and design your own data handling with that same scrutiny in mind.
