## nairobi just became a bigger dot on the map

Digital Realty opened a 6.4MW data center in Nairobi this week, and it's not a vanity project. The facility connects customers to more than 100 networks, which is the kind of interconnection density that actually matters if you're building anything latency-sensitive in East Africa ([Data Center Dynamics](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiowFBVV95cUxPeWhKVjN0ZEdhNTZDVzd6NUNobmRzOHljel9nRU9qM0dBWm9McHpPMmF3TU4tQjNhZUhCc2xxUDlpakhZRFNCTzFST0hjM3o1MzE3NFBmbGp5Y0dZc1MyN0hKRHA4dFRKc0psXzM3VThXbnplbk1idUpocVk1QkFuaU9pUUY3R0ZqcjdBRlNDaldkNUZsUm1aMld5NTNCbjRKZ2dn?oc=5), [GlobeNewswire](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi7wFBVV95cUxNRkhVekVvdVFlZXFEenE5ODZ2RDVTdTlZZnZXR1BOeGJsbzJaVnJOZjk3ZDBvaDZ6dlA0Z2NIRkh0M2VueGNHUUZZMV8ySjNNN1JOUzBhaFRuTjVBdG9ONnM4T0QyVU1NS3V6NldGUF9WcEYzR2dYa1d6U2pPYWNQbE1YSXNDUEpvdUg5dzFNcGJjU0k1TlJhSlBRUlBIZFkyc0wycWlzM2RpdktZVjVud2E4QS1ZamdFNzc5NnktNGJ2M1p6aFFad28yN1lsei1CcTdrYVdva3hQR2JraXRqVkUzYi1ua0dqSkxmbGpxZw?oc=5)). If you build APIs (application programming interfaces) that serve Kenyan users, interconnection sites like this shorten the round trip between your servers and the eyeballs that hit them. That's less abstract than it sounds: fewer network hops between a Nairobi user and your backend usually means fewer timeouts and happier customers.

Nairobi isn't alone. Egypt just signed a $1 billion data center deal, reportedly beating out Chinese bidders for the business ([Business Insider Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi1wFBVV95cUxOMnYtQWp4dW5vbnVzSnNfTkc0VkpSZDlUSkdQTVJBellQMHZ4dXUtQUVvamJKdnpaalNHTGRJcm1HVkpHcVJseXFSMGJ1dnlnQ1hsdXB1U0RJU1prUmdxXzlMR1NLVENFdTlUZVpVOTZ0RV9UVmxnclFldWdvOXZicHRmWnpfcTNEQ2FMRzJQS2tjUmU0SDNFbEtPVk12cE9jWmNnZFpWdnMtR2l6bE9DTUFsUGFJRnVKWVRJMTgtMjFwbjFEZ25nQ3pHbHhUc0NydURWeUo4MA?oc=5)), and Vodafone, Cassava, and Elsewedy Electric have a separate $1 billion project of their own in the country ([Connecting Africa](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitwFBVV95cUxNdlZ5RVZwWko5UTBHU3h0TXkwa3RadC1BNHgtX0lmQjJ4bmVDakhjbHExdGpBUDJzUmUydURWdnNDS3ZTVkJXdkRfdVpscm9tc2F0b2dTcXZ2MzBlSFk0RUM1UERFLUhraFBpZk5aU0hBdFc1Tk80TzZsQzBodXJGczhIVkJ3NzYtelRaVDZ6VGlTUVJTT1ZLRFhrbG1laDNHXzlFTy1SQmxTb2RQa1c2MHNtUjhIWW8?oc=5)). Gulf capital is reportedly circling African AI (artificial intelligence) infrastructure too ([Africa News Agency](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikAFBVV95cUxOdHRBZmhlVHJUSDZHdmFrdlpRT196bWd6aDJia2JSY0VRUEJoZWFtWWpXcnlmRmo0QWNQMWk4NWNxUjNDQUdPamd2X1NJRU9FWlJEVUs3UEl2elg0ZE9KRGVIV01FcTk3b0ZLbmdjTzI4MVZCSEltRmk5cndIQzZJZE9KcnRjZHE2bXN5dDVneXQ?oc=5)). This is the same pattern that made Finland "the data center capital of Europe," with Google, Microsoft, and TikTok pouring over $30.2 billion into the country ([Yahoo Finance](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimwFBVV95cUxPb0JXY0k0RlZsRlRzcmtEOUdjbV8xeGZtMFZFVWNVVnhSdkFNOUh4VVR4OUp3M2FjZDVXanA2NHBhVS1uQ3hJMFdDNHlSWERBUkgtcHlPQzNhSmZ5TUpwNmJTcm12YVl6Nk8wMlJBRWFCeUc3ZWxBdGlhWjJSMXFLNnhPVi1sYzA1YTRnZFJWb0FZTWpNaWcyaS0wcw?oc=5)). Africa is now getting the same investment logic, and it's worth paying attention to as an engineer, not just as a market analyst.

But the boom has a cost side that doesn't show up in press releases. Civil rights groups are urging a halt to South Africa's data center expansion over water and power concerns ([broadbandbreakfast.com](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivgFBVV95cUxOWDlXOFJuZUh1WmRpNnhRWjdlUmhka2Y3WEVlVDFMLWVRU2VRYllEOWNUQlZneFppT25PSTJjQU0xWkJFRFpOMkRtd0s2VHNyN29BdTBYYmszUG1HWWJqakhnWFNETkZxTHk1NUJXZUdQLURuUDFCNHNfeFh5RldDczRhYUkyQ1ZoN3BnbjZjNHRoWWZzanlFSXJUcGlOeGVMWHBrMzI0WndsaXRyeEFfWDU0RXVPUG9ydGh2akxB?oc=5)). If Kenya wants the same wave of investment without the same backlash, the infrastructure planning conversation needs to include water and grid capacity from day one, not as an afterthought once construction is done.

## kenya's regulators are writing the rulebook mid-flight

While the private sector builds first and asks permission later, Kenya's Communications Authority (CA) is trying to catch up. It has opened a 30-day public consultation on a dedicated data center license, its own regulatory category separate from existing telecom rules ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/08/kenya-data-centers-licence-proposal/)). The CA is also consulting on new network equipment rules that touch security, IPv6 (Internet Protocol version 6), AI, e-waste, accessibility, and performance standards all at once ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/kenya-network-equipment-rules-consultation/)). If you work on infrastructure or import networking gear for a Kenyan company, both consultations are worth reading before they close. Rules written without engineering input tend to produce compliance headaches later.

Separately, Kenya launched a 140-page guide to help prosecutors and investigators handle cybercrime cases, covering digital evidence preservation and case building ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/08/kenya-cybercrime-guide-launch/)). That's a good sign if you've ever tried to report a hacked account or fraud case to local authorities and hit a wall of officers who didn't know how to handle digital evidence.

Not every rollout is going smoothly. KEMIS (Kenya Education Management Information System), the replacement for NEMIS (National Education Management Information System), is hitting technical problems that are colliding with Grade 10 school selection and raising concerns about learner records and funding ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/the-nemis-to-kemis-dilemma/)). It's a reminder that migrating a national system of record is a genuinely hard engineering problem, and cutting over before the data pipeline is solid has real consequences for real families.

## agentic AI shows up in cybersecurity, for better and worse

At Fal.Con 2026 in Las Vegas, NVIDIA's Jensen Huang and CrowdStrike's George Kurtz announced CrowdStrike SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system built to counter automated attacks with automated defense ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-crowdstrike-fal-con-2026/)). The framing is that attacks are automated now, so defense has to be too. Fair enough, but it's also worth remembering that "agentic" security tooling introduces its own attack surface: an autonomous agent with access to your infrastructure is a new thing to threat-model, not just a new thing to trust.

On the consumer side of security, Google has made it easier to move passwords and passkeys between password managers on Android without exporting unencrypted files, closing a gap that used to force people into insecure workarounds when switching apps ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/android-password-manager-transfer/)). Meanwhile, an investigation reportedly found LG smart TVs scanning home networks and collecting device data even when supposedly off ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/lg-smart-tvs-network-scanning-privacy/)). Put those two stories side by side and you get the current state of consumer security: better password hygiene tools on one hand, more devices quietly phoning home on the other.
