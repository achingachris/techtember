## kenya's own digital plumbing had a rough week too

zoom in on kenya's policy side and the theme holds: infrastructure ambition outrunning execution. the communications authority (CA) opened public consultation on new network equipment rules touching security, IPv6, AI, e-waste, accessibility, and performance standards ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/kenya-network-equipment-rules-consultation/)), worth reading closely if you import networking gear. meanwhile KEMIS, the system replacing NEMIS as kenya's learner records platform, is hitting technical problems colliding with grade 10 school selection, the kind of migration pain you'd recognize if you've ever cut over a production database with live users still on it ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/the-nemis-to-kemis-dilemma/)). kenya's cyber attacks are also reportedly up 6 percent as ransomware nearly doubles globally, context that makes nvidia and crowdstrike's fal.con 2026 announcement of safemind, an agentic cybersecurity system, read less like a keynote flex and more like an admission that automated attacks now need automated defense ([nvidia](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-crowdstrike-fal-con-2026/)).

## the chatbot got recruited, and everyone noticed

the security thread that ran through every single edition this week: anthropic's own threat report on claude, its large language model (LLM), details criminals, spies, propagandists, and even rival AI labs trying to weaponize it ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/anthopic-claude-threat-report-ai-misuse/)). the kenya-specific case is the one worth sitting with: anthropic says an operator used claude to mass-produce political posts designed to look like organic grassroots opinion ahead of the 2027 elections ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/kenya-ai-claude-political-propaganda/)). if you ship anything with generative text at scale in kenya's civic or media space, treat astroturfing detection as a launch requirement, not a post-incident feature request. on a calmer note, google made it easier for android users to transfer passwords and passkeys between password managers without an unencrypted export sitting in a downloads folder ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/10/android-password-manager-transfer/)), and reporting suggested LG smart TVs may be scanning home networks and collecting device data even when supposedly off, a good reason to keep smart TVs on their own VLAN or guest network ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/lg-smart-tvs-network-scanning-privacy/)).

## apple finally folded, and mathematicians finally pushed back

apple shipped its first foldable, the iphone duo, at $1,999 with a 7.6 inch display and split view multitasking, alongside the iphone 18 pro and pro max with variable aperture cameras and a new A20 pro chip, plus airpods 5, watch series 12, and watch ultra 4 ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/iphone-duo-foldable-phone/), [techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/iphone-18-pro-and-max-launch/), [techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/09/apple-airpods-5-watch-series-12-health-features/)). the detail every edition kept circling back to: apple reportedly pays samsung about $250 per folding screen, which explains most of that $1,999 price tag and confirms foldables remain a component-cost problem before they're a software problem ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/11/iphone-duo-folding-screen-cost/)).

then, right at the end of the week, openai claimed its agents solved the ninety-year-old navier-stokes problem, a foundational question in fluid dynamics. mathematicians are not convinced, citing unanswered questions about training data provenance and a lack of a transparent, peer-reviewable proof ([techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/09/14/openai-navier-stokes-proof/)). treat that one as unresolved: a confident-sounding output is not a verified result, and the burden of proof doesn't transfer to the model just because it sounds sure of itself.

## the week in one sentence

capital moves fast toward compute that scales globally (data centers, foldable screens, agentic security tools) and moves slowly, or backward, on infrastructure that only makes sense locally (fiber, grid access, learner record migrations). plan your architecture around that gap, not around the press releases.
